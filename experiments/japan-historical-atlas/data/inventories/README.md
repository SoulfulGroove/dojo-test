# Japan, 1580–1650 · Master historical inventory

**Edition 1.0 · Six AI research reports · 196 entries**

This is a human-readable version of three separate, machine-readable inventories for the historical Japan atlas. It contains exactly the same entries, identifiers and report references as the JSON files. These are **candidate historically supported items derived from the supplied reports**, not a newly verified primary-source database.

## Reading the evidence labels

- **D · Document-linked in report:** The report connects this item to a named historical diary, official record, text, or period source. **The specific original passage was not independently inspected or verified when building these lists.**
- **S · Synthesized:** Included in one or more reports as a historically grounded scholarly or regional synthesis, without a checked original passage for the specific item.
- **P · Provisional:** Candidate requiring further confirmation of period, origin, locality, occupational specialization, or evidence.

**Multiple report codes do not imply independently corroborating primary documents.** If a source is silent about a resource or occupation in one place, that does not establish absence there. Broad English role names are descriptive, not necessarily original Japanese job titles. The `origin_context` fields distinguish imported or uncertain-origin goods and materials from domestic production claims.

## Evidence and data design

- A resource means a source input (mineral, land product, marine organism, animal, or raw material), while processed goods record useful products of labor; some transitional goods such as spun fibre and imported raw silk are documented in the processed-goods inventory.
- The work of a person is recorded independently of the materials they might have used. For example, the existence of the job “blacksmith” does not prove a mine at that same locality.
- The JSON records use stable `R###`, `G###`, and `O###` IDs and fields for category, report mentions, evidence anchor and provisional evidence class. Record IDs do **not** encode province, date, or proven domestic origin.
- There is **no assertion** of item presence at every point in 1580–1650 or within any geographic region. Before attaching something to the map, add a location-specific, dated claim and independently checked source citation.
- These inventory lists intentionally do **not** mix in locations, routes, inferred supply dependencies, or negative-resource assertions. Those are distinct relationship tables for a later iteration.

### Proposed next database layer

```text
resource R### ──[used_to_make]──> product G###
occupation O### ──[produces / transports / uses]──> product G###
location L### ──[produces / imports / documented_absent / unknown]──> R### or G###
```

Each relationship needs a period, source ID, exact citation, certainty, and note. Unknown is the default: **lack of evidence is not evidence of lack**.
