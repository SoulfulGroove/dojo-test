About these layers
Modern map: Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (GSI).
Historical source: Lex Berman, Harvard Dataverse, Japan Tokugawa GIS (2017).
Display: Province outlines become finer when zoomed out. Tiny detached triangular source fragments are hidden by default; use “Tiny fragments” to show them. GeoJSON export always includes every original fragment.
Caution: Harvard's province GIS is based largely on later Edo reference geography (around 1828), not verified 1580–1650 borders. Kuni are historical provinces, not modern prefectures or daimyo domains. Northern and Ryukyu political geographies require separate treatment.
Historical boundary shapes are research approximations. Modern shorelines also differ from those in the early 1600s. No towns, roads, or resource claims have been added.
