What makes a game action possible?

      
To give the villager water, the player must have the cup (C), it must contain water (W), and the villager must not already be helped (¬H).

    Actions and their consequences
    
      
    
      
    Arrows represent possible causal progression, not a mandatory click order. Rule prerequisites are evaluated on the current state. Actions may set or clear state variables.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    A subtle but essential distinction
    
      
    Guard: C ∧ W ∧ ¬H tells us when the action may execute.
    
      
    Effect: Giving the water sets H and opens the gate, while consuming the cup.
    
      
    Therefore, gateOpen does not mean the guard is still true. After a successful handoff the guard normally becomes false. A dependency chart is not just a list of truth-equivalent propositions; it also represents state-changing events.

      Why this matters for Hiragana Dojo
      
      
      The game can deliver clues through Japanese dialogue, signs, and observations. The deduction engine checks logical consistency, while a separate learning layer records whether players understood the Japanese independently or needed help.
      
      
      This is an original English-language deduction demonstration, not an implemented Japanese-language lesson. The constraint rules are evaluated by enumerating all possible pairings, not by a full SAT or CSP solver library.
